## Background

XPOOL: on the owner's host, pools of slow disks writing in bulk slow down a fast pool's reads and synchronous writes. The bench (`run.sh qv`) reproduces it in memory: the fast pool `fastq` (a mirror of two 1 GiB null_blk devices, 4K records, `sync=always`) next to three slow raidz2 pools `slow1`-`slow3` (six 128 MiB null_blk devices each under dm-delay, 8 ms), each taking a 1 MiB sequential writer. Run 2 measures the fast pool's rows rr1 and rr4 (4K random reads, 1 and 4 jobs) and sw1 and sw4 (4K random `O_SYNC` writes), 20 s each, while the slow pools write; Single measures sw1 and sw4 with the fast pool alone.

This is the last XPOOL measurement (register V-05). Every other XPOOL candidate is closed on a measured reason (TASK-1120-X3 and TASK-1120-X4 on this VM, 21/11). Two leads are still open, and the owner's rule is that each is followed to the end and closed only on a measured reason:

- **c1**: synchronous writes in their own high-priority write issue taskq (`zio_taskq_write` = `sync scale scale null`, 2 taskqs x 3 threads at nice −20, instead of the front of `z_wr_iss`). TASK-1116-X2 on this VM: m3w-c1 sw1 IOPS +116 % in Run 2, +22 % alone, no read or slow-pool cost. It was set aside on 17/11 for the ZIL inline-issue track (now **v10a**: a ZIL block is issued by the thread that writes it out, not by `z_wr_iss`), which never ran on this bench. On this bench a ZIL block carries sw1's and sw4's writes, so with v10a c1's taskq may have little left to carry. TASK-1116-C1B's row n8i (64K `WR_INDIRECT` writes, the #16146 shape, where `dmu_sync()` writes still go through the taskq) gave c1 −5 % against master (7732 against 8138, p 0.24), its high taskq saturated.
- **C2**: synchronous read completions in their own interrupt taskq (`zio_taskq_read` = `fixed,1,8 null scale scale`), a code change of the prototype (48aff83c567) never measured alone: every arm of X, X2, X3 and X4 kept master's read row.

**The code under test** (`vm-code-x5`, 24 commits on upstream master `1b1aa0e91b6`):

## Written before the run: the rules that decide

From the XPOOL conclusion of 22/11, verbatim:

1. c1 on top of v10a: m3w-zi-c1 vs m3w-zi has sw1 or sw4 IOPS beyond the spread above, no p99/p99.9 beyond the spread above on any Run 2 row, slow MiB/s not beyond the spread below, and alone-zi-c1 not beyond the spread below alone-zi -> c1 stays a default-change candidate, go to Part B; otherwise c1 is closed ("v10a covers it").

How `xp-report.py` applies them (fixed here too, before the run): "beyond the spread" = the two [min, max] ranges over the passes do not overlap, percentages compare means; "any Run 2 row" and "any row" = rr1, rr4, sw1 and sw4; "alone-zi-c1 not beyond the spread below alone-zi" = Single's sw1 and sw4 IOPS; "m3w" in rules 3 and 4 = the baseline (m3w and m3w-up2 together, as in X3 and X4; m3w alone if m3w-up2 ran in fewer than 2 passes); in rule 4 a loss is IOPS beyond the spread below, or p50, p99 or p99.9 beyond the spread above. Rule 2: per pass, ratio = v10a-c1 IOPS / v10a IOPS on n8i; "significantly below" = geometric mean of the ratios below 1 and the exact two-sided sign-flip permutation p (V7P to V10P's) below 0.05; with fewer than 6 passes where both arms ran, the test cannot reach p < 0.05 and rule 2 is "not decided". `xp-report.py` prints each rule's figures and result; quote them and recompute none by eye.
