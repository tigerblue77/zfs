Don't cancel a rebuild over a leaf that is being reopened

Once per metaslab, the sequential resilver (rebuild) thread asks
vdev_resilver_needed() whether any leaf still needs it, and cancels
itself when none does.  vdev_resilver_needed() only counts writeable
leaves, and the thread holds only SCL_CONFIG while asking.
vdev_reopen() runs with SCL_STATE_ALL held as writer, closes every
leaf and only then opens them again, so a check that lands in between
finds the new leaf closed and cancels the rebuild.  Nothing restarts
it: the replacing vdev stays, and "zpool wait" never returns.

Every "zpool scrub" reopens all vdevs before it checks whether it may
start, so a scrub that is refused because a resilver is running is
enough to trigger this.  So is "zpool reopen", and "zpool online" of a
device of the same top-level vdev.  That is how
functional/replacement/scrub_cancel hung on a CI VM until it was
killed after 600 seconds: it refuses a scrub right after
"zpool replace -s".  Replaying that test in userspace (libzpool, file
vdevs), the rebuild was canceled and the replace hung once in 152
iterations.

Hold SCL_STATE as reader around the check, as vdev_rebuild_range()
already does around its own vdev_writeable() check before it issues
I/O.  vdev_rebuild_should_cancel() is only called by the rebuild
thread, with SCL_CONFIG held and vdev_rebuild_lock not held, so the
lock order is unchanged.

rebuild_reopen runs sequential resilvers on vdevs with many metaslabs
while scrubs are requested in a loop, and fails if one is canceled or
does not finish in time.  Replayed in userspace, it failed in each of
12 runs on master, at the first rebuild, and passed in each of 12
with this change.
