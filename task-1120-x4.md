## Background

XPOOL: on the owner's host, pools of slow disks writing in bulk slow down a fast pool's reads and synchronous writes. The bench (`run.sh qv`) reproduces it in memory: the fast pool `fastq` (a mirror of two 1 GiB null_blk devices) next to three slow raidz2 pools `slow1`-`slow3` (six 128 MiB null_blk devices each under dm-delay, 8 ms), each taking a 1 MiB sequential writer. Run 2 measures the fast pool's rows rr1 and rr4 (4K random reads, 1 and 4 jobs) and sw1 and sw4 (4K random `O_SYNC` writes), 20 s each, while the slow pools write.

TASK-1116-X2 on this VM (16/11, 4 passes; "beyond the spread" = the two [min, max] ranges over the passes do not overlap; percentages compare means; baseline = m3w + m3w-up2):

- **m3w-c1** (`zio_taskq_write` = `sync scale scale null`): sw1 IOPS +116 %, nothing on rr1 or rr4, no cost.
- **m3w-lowpri** (c1 + `zio_taskq_bulk_lowpri=1`: the write issue taskqs `z_wr_iss` and `s_wr_iss` of every pool at nice 0 instead of −19) against m3w-c1: rr1 IOPS +65 %, p99 −71 %; rr4 IOPS +52 %, p99 −81 %; sw4 p99 +17 %; slow-pool write MiB/s −13 % (101.3 against 115.8). Against the baseline, no p99 or p99.9 rise on any row.
- **m3w-yield** (c1 + `zio_taskq_bulk_yield=1`) alone: no read gain.
- **m3w-w2** (c1 + lowpri + yield) against m3w-c1: the read gains, plus a write-tail rise (sw1 p99.9 +44 %, sw4 p99 +49 %, p99.9 +30 %) that needs lowpri and yield together; alone-w2 (no other pool) does not show it.
- **m3w-w2 rr1 against the baseline: +44 %** (11393 against 7920 IOPS, p99 1882 us). On 15/11 (TASK-1115-X, same modules, srcversion `BD9507FD52CD064F29D2297`) the same arm gave **+76 %** (14764 against 8367, p99 430 us).
- Controls (sched.json): `slow_iss` cpus in sw4 0.49 (c1) to 0.44 (lowpri); `fio` wait_us in rr1 78 to 36.

c1 as a default was dropped on 17/11 (TASK-1116-C1B; the ZIL issue track was chosen instead); here c1 stays the prototype's knob set that every arm but m3w and m3w-up2 builds on, as in X2.

Open questions this task answers:

- **(b)** An intermediate nice (between −19 and 0) for the bulk write issue threads: does it keep most of lowpri's read gain with less of its slow-pool cost?
- **(c)** Exempting a pool whose txgs a thread waits for from the demotion: does it remove w2's write-tail rise and keep its read gain? And does such a waiter exist on the fast pool during the sw rows at all?
- **(d)** lowpri has no tail cost of its own: is its slow-pool cost from the slow pools' own nice level, from the fast pool's demotion, or from the extra work of a faster fast pool?
- **(e)** Why w2's rr1 gain changed from +76 % to +44 %: what the runs recorded, side by side.

**The code under test** (both parameters off by default; while they keep their defaults nothing changes but four new per-pool counters):

- `zio_taskq_bulk_pri_pct` (uint, default 100) places the taskqs that `zio_taskq_bulk_lowpri` lowers at that percentage of the way from the I/O taskqs' priority (0 = maxclsyspri, nice −20) to the default priority (100 = defclsyspri, nice 0). On Linux, nice = pct / 5 − 20: 50 is nice −10, 75 is nice −5. Read when a pool's taskqs are created, at import.
- Per pool, the threads blocked in `txg_wait_synced_flags()` or `txg_wait_open()` are counted in two kinds: *restart* (a writer that `dmu_tx_wait()` holds back for want of space or dirty data room, and the free throttle of `dmu_free_long_range()`) and *explicit* (everything else: a ZIL writer that cannot allocate a log block, a sync task, `zpool` commands, an fsync that falls back to a txg). `zio_taskq_write_waited` (uint, default 0, read at every dispatch): with 2, while any thread waits for a pool's txgs, that pool's asynchronous writes go to its high-priority write issue taskq (`z_wr_iss_h` / `s_wr_iss_h`: nice −20, no yield) instead of the bulk one; with 1, only while an explicit waiter exists. Only with c1 (the high-priority taskq must exist).
- Four counters at the end of every pool's `/proc/spl/kstat/zfs/<pool>/iostats`, cumulative from the pool's import, counting in every arm whatever the parameter: `txg_wait_explicit_count` (explicit waits entered), `txg_wait_restart_count` (restart waits entered), `tx_dirty_wait_count` (writers held at the dirty data limit or delayed by the write throttle; diagnostic only, they never trigger the exemption), `waited_async_write_count` (asynchronous writes the exemption sent to the high-priority taskq).

The build also holds TASK-1120-X3's write issue budget (`zio_taskq_write_budget`, 0 here in every arm), as the X4 tree of 16/11 did.

**What changed from TASK-1116-X4 (16/11, never run):** the code is no longer two base64 patches applied to X3's tree: the prototype (`perf-xpool-cause` `6549ab63bd8`), the budget (`gate.patch`) and the two patches (`bulk-pri.patch` sha256 `06cf31df…`, `write-waited.patch` sha256 `3f4521ed…`, now commits) were rebased on upstream master `1b1aa0e91b6`, with no conflict outside the workflow files the prototype deletes, and are built fresh in this task's own tree; the bench as X4 would have run it (run.sh sha256 `1997156b…`), the CPU probe, a driver and a report come from a parentless kit commit.
